BRIDGE: Bridging the Semantic Quantization Gap for Generative Recommendation

Yatong Xiao、Mingjie Sun、Peng Wang、Wenjin Wu 等 5 人

英文摘要

Generative recommendation retrieves items by autoregressively generating Semantic IDs(SIDs), where an RQ-VAE quantizes each item into a hierarchical SID sequence that the decoder generates level by level. We identify an overlooked bottleneck in this paradigm: the Semantic Quantization Gap(SQG). Quantization derives SIDs from rich continuous signals, including codebook centroids, local neighborhood geometry, and level-wise entropy. However, the decoder receives only discrete identifiers and loses the semantic structure that shaped them. This mismatch manifests in three ways: a grounding gap, where the decoder lacks semantic anchors when target-side information is limited; a neighborhood gap, where token-level supervision discards inter-item similarity and exacerbates exposure bias; and a difficulty gap, where computation is allocated uniformly despite generation difficulty diminishing with depth. To bridge the SQG, we propose BRIDGE. Codebook-grounded latent reasoning injects frozen quantizer centroids as semantic anchors before SID generation. Privileged EMA self-distillation transfers neighborhood-aware knowledge from a teacher to an on-policy student, preserving inter-item geometry while mitigating exposure bias. Effort-aware recurrence allocates more computation to the challenging early levels and progressively reduces it with depth. Across three Amazon benchmarks, BRIDGE improves Recall@10 and NDCG@10 by 10.9% and 9.7% on average over the strongest baseline (up to 12.6% and 10.7%). In a one-week online A/B experiment on a large-scale e-commerce advertising platform with 5% traffic, BRIDGE improves eCPM by 0.65%.

Beyond Independent Predictions: Learning the Browsing Journey for Sequence-Level Re-ranking

Jiayi Zhang、Jingxin Hai、Zhenjia Ma、Hongbin Pei 等 7 人

英文摘要

Re-ranking aims to present users with an ordered list that maximizes sequence-level utility. Industrial deployments widely adopt the Generator--Evaluator framework, where the generator proposes candidate sequences, and the evaluator scores them to select the best one for display. The fidelity of the evaluator's utility estimates thus directly governs recommendation quality. However, existing evaluators predominantly follow the \textbf{Independent Behavior Modeling (IBM)} paradigm, which simplifies the continuous user \emph{browsing journey} into independent per-item predictions (e.g., CTR). This oversimplification ignores two physical realities: (i) a click can occur only after the item has been reached and impressed, and (ii) items compete for finite user attention. As a result, IBM produces systematically biased evaluations and suboptimal sequence selection. To address this, we propose a \textbf{Joint Behavior Modeling (JBM)} perspective that explicitly models the \emph{display} $\rightarrow$ \emph{impression} $\rightarrow$ \emph{click} process to capture causal and competitive dependencies within a sequence. Building on JBM, we develop the \textbf{Impression-Click Evaluator (ICE)}, a plug-and-play framework that (i) predicts a browsing-depth distribution to estimate per-position impression probabilities, (ii) leverages predicted impressions to suppress spurious context from unexposed items during click modeling, and (iii) introduces a click distribution matching objective to capture intra-sequence dependencies. Extensive offline experiments across diverse backbones, together with a 14-day online A/B test in the Taobao App (+0.70\% clicks, +3.67\% revenue) and full-traffic deployment, validate the effectiveness and robustness of our approach.

TACT-KV: Tri-Axis Cosine Transform for Compressing Volumetric KV Caches in Medical VLMs

Chongyu Qu、Ritchie Zhao、Yufan He、Dong Yang 等 15 人

英文摘要

Vision-language models (VLMs) applied to 3D medical scans face a structural memory wall. A single CT or MRI volume produces tens of thousands of vision tokens, and the resulting key-value (KV) cache scales linearly with both batch and context. Existing KV compression methods operate on the cache's feature or token statistics, or apply 1D and 2D transforms, leaving the three-axis spatial structure in the cache unexploited. We propose TACT-KV (Tri-Axis Cosine Transform), a calibration-free KV compression method that exploits strong spatial correlations in visual KV states along all three anatomical axes of 3D scans. This correlation concentrates the KV cache energy under a frequency-domain transform. TACT-KV applies a separable 3D discrete cosine transform to the KV cache, allocates a per-layer bit budget across frequency bins via reverse water-filling (more bits to high-energy components), and quantizes each bin with a fixed scalar codebook. We derive a close-form relation: the distortion reduction from adaptive bit allocation is dominated by the spectral concentration of the transformed cache. Across six medical VLMs and two 3D CT benchmarks, TACT-KV achieves the lowest 1-bit prediction-flip rate on eleven of twelve model and dataset pairs in our main comparison. The same advantage reproduces on four general-purpose VLMs evaluated on video tasks, indicating that the benefit of exploiting three-axis structure generalizes beyond medical imaging. At equal high-bandwidth memory (HBM), TACT-KV scales the decoding batch size on a single GPU by up to $8\times$.

CacheGraft: Enhancing Retrieval-Augmented Generation via Latent-Space Document Enrichment

Vihan Lakshman、Peter Baile Chen、Zhuohan Gu、Michael Cafarella

英文摘要

Recent work in machine learning has demonstrated that transformer-based language model agents can communicate state not only through text, but also directly in latent space via key-value (KV) caches. In this paper, we argue that such cache-to-cache translation can provide utility beyond the original motivating application of real-time agent communication and can instead be utilized for retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). Building on the Cache-to-Cache (C2C) fusion framework, our proposed system CacheGraft encodes each corpus passage offline into a fused KV cache representation between a larger sharer model (4B parameters in our experiments) and a smaller receiver model (0.6B parameters) such that this cache can be injected into the receiver at query time with no need for the larger model. Across five QA benchmarks and two independent locally-run judges, CacheGraft improves answer quality over the text-only receiver baseline in 29 out of 30 comparisons, of which 17 of the 30 are statistically significant. Notably, these gains are zero-shot with respect to the fuser, which was never trained on retrieval data. These initial results suggest that latent-space document enrichment may be a promising avenue for enhancing the performance of RAG systems, particularly in compute-constrained settings where it is only feasible to deploy a small model at inference time.

Muir: Linear Attention with Sensitivity-Allocated Gating and Matched-Filter Readout

Tushaar Gangavarapu、Gil Tabak、Clemens JS Schaefer

英文摘要

Linear attention compresses context into a fixed-size key-value state for linear-time training and constant-memory inference. Gated delta-rule models decay this state and erase its prediction for an incoming key before writing a new value; Gated DeltaNet-2 (GDN-2) generalizes this with channel-wise decay and decoupled, full-rank erase and write gates. However, we show that state updates are far less sensitive to erasure than to writes: key activation and unit normalization concentrate key energy onto a sparse subset of channels, whereas value writes scale all channels, so the erase gate can be parameterized at lower rank. Furthermore, slow-decaying state rows accumulate much larger norms than fast rows, dominating unweighted readout and forcing stronger write-time erasure to suppress interference. Guided by this analysis, we introduce Muir, which fuses all gates into a sensitivity-allocated low-rank projection, re-weights state rows with a matched-filter readout gate, and reinvests the saved parameters in a wider feed-forward network. Because standard chunkwise algorithms become numerically unstable at the large chunk sizes needed to saturate wide matrix units, we design a parallel update for stable, efficient training. Across 166M-1.34B parameters, Muir lowers perplexity by 1.8-2.0% over GDN-2 and 2.5-3.6% over Transformer, raises commonsense accuracy by up to 1.9 points, and improves extractive recall over GDN-2. At 1.34B, Muir outperforms both baselines on multi-hop and multi-needle recall, extrapolates 2,560x from its 4K training length to 10M-token streams without perplexity drift, and maintains near-100% passkey retrieval to 1M tokens with static readout. Muir achieves these gains while matching GDN-2 throughput, reaching its 100B-token validation loss 1.2x faster, and training 2.7x faster than Transformer at 32K.

GINGER: Spicing Up Graph-Based Vector Search

Daniel Salwasser、Lars Gottesbüren、Laxman Dhulipala、Jakub Łącki 等 10 人

英文摘要

We present \textsc{Ginger}, a state-of-the-art open-source C++ library for graph-based approximate nearest neighbor search (ANNS). We developed \textsc{Ginger} at Google to push the speed and memory efficiency of graph search on large and high-dimensional embedding datasets. \textsc{Ginger} surpasses existing ANNS libraries with regards to query throughput and indexing time while matching the memory footprint of the most space-efficient competitors. \textsc{Ginger} builds upon recent advances in proximity graph construction (PiPNN) and vector quantization (TurboQuant). We introduce Nested TurboQuant (NTQ), a two-level quantizer which supports low precision graph navigation and high precision reranking in a single code. This representation achieves near floating point precision with $2.6\times$ less memory. We also compress the size of our proximity graphs by up to $2.5\times$ without impacting query throughput. We combine graph reordering with a new lossless compression codec called AdaptiveGap, which builds upon StreamVByte. At $95\%$ recall, \textsc{Ginger} speeds up query throughput by an average of $1.8\times$ over each dataset's fastest competitor and by at least $2.3\times$ over any single library. This lead grows to $2.9\times$ over compact libraries on high-dimensional embeddings.
